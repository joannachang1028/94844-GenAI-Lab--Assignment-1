### Full Case Report Text

**Title:** Laparoscopic low anterior resection for rectal cancer with rectal prolapse: a case report  

**Authors:** Ryusei Yamamoto, Yasuji Mokuno, Hideo Matsubara, Hirokazu Kaneko, Shinsuke Iyomasa  

**Affiliation:** Department of Surgery, Yachiyo Hospital, Anjo-shi, Aichi, Japan  

---

### Abstract  
**Background:** Rectal cancer with rectal prolapse is rare, and laparoscopic low anterior resection (Lap-LAR) for this condition has not been reported previously.  
**Case presentation:** A 63-year-old Japanese woman presented with complete rectal prolapse and rectal bleeding. Examination revealed a 7-cm soft, ulcerated tumor 5 cm from the anal verge. Biopsy confirmed adenocarcinoma. Laparoscopic low anterior resection using a prolapsing technique (without rectopexy) achieved clear margins. The patient recovered uneventfully and remained recurrence-free after 12 months.  
**Conclusions:** Lap-LAR without rectopexy is a feasible and effective approach for rectal cancer with rectal prolapse.  

---

### Case Presentation  
The patient had two years of rectal prolapse and bleeding.  
- **Findings:** Complete prolapse with soft ulcerated tumor (7 × 6 cm).  
- **Lab:** Hb 5.9 g/dL; normal CEA/CA19-9.  
- **Imaging:** CT and enema showed lower rectal tumor; no metastasis.  
- **Diagnosis:** cT1N0M0.  

**Surgical procedure:** Laparoscopic low anterior resection using the prolapsing technique. The distal rectum was everted, resected under direct vision (1.5 cm margin), and anastomosed using double-stapling. Operative time 194 min; blood loss 10 mL.  

**Pathology:** Moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma invading submucosa (pT1N0M0).  

**Postoperative course:** Uneventful; no incontinence or recurrence at 12 months.  

---

### Discussion  
Lap-LAR without rectopexy provides oncologic adequacy and functional preservation for rectal cancer with prolapse. The prolapsing technique allows precise distal margin control and avoids tumor cell seeding. Rectopexy may be unnecessary, as fibrosis after anastomosis stabilizes the rectum.  

The etiology suggests large rectal tumors may lead to prolapse via intussusception. Patients with rectal prolapse have higher colorectal cancer risk and should be screened.  

---

### Conclusion  
Laparoscopic low anterior resection using the prolapsing technique is effective for treating rectal cancer with rectal prolapse, offering oncologic safety and good functional outcomes without rectopexy.
