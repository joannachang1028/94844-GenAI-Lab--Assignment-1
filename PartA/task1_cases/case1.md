**Title:** Rectal cancer with situs inversus totalis and previous malignant middle cerebral artery infarction: a case report and review of the literature

### Abstract  
**Background:** Situs inversus totalis (SIT) is a rare congenital condition involving complete inversion of thoracic and abdominal organs. Limited evidence exists regarding laparoscopic treatment of rectal cancer in patients with SIT and additional comorbidities.  
**Case presentation:** A 61-year-old Chinese male with rectal cancer, SIT, malignant middle cerebral artery (MCA) infarction, carotid artery stenosis, and diabetes underwent successful laparoscopic radical resection of rectal cancer. The operation proceeded without complications, and he was discharged with good recovery. Pathology confirmed T3N0M0 stage disease, and 24-month follow-up showed no recurrence.  
**Conclusions:** With adequate preoperative preparation and multidisciplinary planning, surgical treatment of rectal cancer can be safely performed in patients with SIT and complex comorbidities.  

---

### Case Presentation  
A 61-year-old male presented with abdominal distension and mucous-bloody stool for one week.  
- **History:** Large cerebral infarction (2018) with left-leg paralysis; type 2 diabetes; carotid artery stenosis.  
- **Examination:** Apical pulsation on right side; distended abdomen; left-leg paralysis.  
- **Laboratory findings:** Hb 72 g/L; platelets 470 × 10⁹/L; glucose 6.52 mmol/L; normal tumor markers.  
- **Imaging:** CT revealed situs inversus totalis, multiple cerebral infarctions, and rectal mass; CTA confirmed severe right carotid and MCA stenosis.  
- **Endoscopy:** Rectal adenocarcinoma, 15 cm from anus; cT3N0M0.  

**Surgical treatment:**  
Laparoscopic radical rectal resection (Hartmann procedure) was performed with modified positioning due to mirror-image anatomy. The operation lasted 3 hours with 40 mL blood loss. The patient recovered well.  

**Pathology:** Moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma, stage T3N0M0.  

**Follow-up:** At 24 months, no recurrence or metastasis was observed.  

---

### Discussion  
SIT occurs in 1:6,500–25,000 individuals and can complicate surgery due to mirrored anatomy. For rectal cancer in SIT patients with severe vascular disease, preoperative imaging and multidisciplinary management are essential. This case highlights safe laparoscopic radical resection using careful planning and mirror-adapted techniques.  

---

### Conclusion  
Rectal cancer with SIT and cerebrovascular comorbidities can be treated safely through laparoscopic surgery when supported by detailed imaging and multidisciplinary care.
