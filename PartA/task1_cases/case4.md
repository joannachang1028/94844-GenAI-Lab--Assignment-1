### Abstract  
**Introduction:** Rectal cancer with rectovesical fistula is a rare and difficult-to-treat condition. We describe a case successfully managed through multimodality therapy, including neoadjuvant chemoradiation and total pelvic exenteration. To the best of our knowledge, this was the first documented case of its kind.  
**Case presentation:** A 51-year-old Chinese man was diagnosed with rectal adenocarcinoma complicated by rectovesical fistula. He received neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, followed by total pelvic exenteration (TPE) and adjuvant chemotherapy. Postoperative pathology confirmed a complete pathological response (pCR).  
**Conclusions:** This case demonstrates that proactive multidisciplinary management can achieve complete cure for locally advanced rectal cancer with rectovesical fistula.

### Case Presentation  
A 51-year-old Asian man presented with increased stool frequency (6–8 times per day), tenesmus, fecaluria, and severe weight loss.  
**Examination:** Digital rectal exam revealed a protruding anterior rectal wall mass; colonoscopy confirmed a 4 cm ulcerated lesion 4 cm from the anal verge.  
**Pathology:** Moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma.  
**Imaging:**  
- CT: Rectal tumor invading bladder.  
- Cystoscopy: 6×6 cm ulcerative mass at bladder neck.  
- MRI: Lesion penetrating bladder with visible fistula; multiple mesorectal lymph nodes (T4bN2M0, Stage IIIC).  

Given infection and obstruction, immediate resection was unsafe. A multidisciplinary tumor board recommended **transverse colostomy**, followed by **neoadjuvant therapy** once stabilized.  

**Neoadjuvant regimen:**  
- Oxaliplatin (80 mg weekly) + Capecitabine (1.5 g twice daily) for 4 weeks  
- Concurrent IMRT: 50.6 Gy to gross tumor volume (GTV) and 41.8 Gy to clinical target volume (CTV).
